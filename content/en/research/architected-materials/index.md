Architected materials obtain their properties from geometry rather than from composition alone. We use this freedom to design materials whose mechanical response is **programmable**: prescribed in advance, and often strongly nonlinear.

Two design concepts recur in our work. **Layered architectures** let a structure be assembled from simple constituents while exhibiting a response none of them has individually. **Graded porosity** gives a continuous handle on local stiffness, so that deformation can be distributed through a structure instead of concentrated.

## What we work on

- **Extreme and precisely controlled nonlinear responses.** Inverse design of layered and graded architectures that deliver a target force–displacement curve, including responses that are difficult to obtain with a homogeneous material.
- **Multistability and switchable states.** Structures that hold more than one stable configuration, and whose state can be switched predictably under load or actuation.
- **Buckling as a design mechanism.** Treating buckling — usually a failure mode — as a controlled, tunable deformation mode, and validating the predicted behaviour experimentally.
- **Extremal effective properties.** Microstructure design at and near the bounds of the attainable elastic property space.
